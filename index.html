01 · FOUNDATIONS

What is a new gTLD?

A top-level domain (TLD) is the part of an Internet domain name to the right of the final dot. Familiar examples include .com and .org. A new gTLD is a top-level domain introduced through an ICANN application round.

Depending on the application and applicable rules, a string can relate to a brand, geographic name, community, or generic term. The distinction between a domain name and a TLD is fundamental: in www.example.brand, “.brand” is the TLD while “example.brand” is the domain name.

02 · CONTEXT

Why the 2026 Round matters

01

Years of policy work

The round follows extensive ICANN community policy development and implementation work that shaped the program’s rules and procedures.

02

More linguistic possibilities

The program supports applications involving Internationalized Domain Name (IDN) strings, enabling top-level domains in multiple scripts subject to the applicable requirements.

03

A defined application process

Applicants proceed through a structured sequence of submission, evaluation and, where applicable, contention, contracting and technical onboarding.

04 · PROCESS

The applicant journey

The exact path varies by application type and circumstances. Some stages and procedures apply only in specific cases.

01

Prepare

Assess eligibility, string considerations, documentation, resources and operational plans.

02

Submit

Submit the application and required information through TAMS.

03

Pre-evaluation

Public application information, string replacement where applicable, and confirmation steps.

04

Evaluate

String, applicant and application evaluations, plus applicable community input, objections or appeals.

05

Resolve contention

Where applicable, contention is addressed through the procedures established by the program.

06

Contract

Successful applicants enter into the 2026 Base Registry Agreement with ICANN.

07

Onboard & delegate

Technical onboarding and testing precede delegation and operation of the gTLD.

06 · DUE DILIGENCE

Key application considerations

Applicant eligibility

Organizational, financial and other eligibility requirements apply.

Proposed string

String rules, reserved names, geographic names, similarity and IDN considerations may apply.

Technical operations

Registry systems, DNS, security, continuity and technical testing are part of the operational framework.

Financial requirements

Applicants must account for applicable program fees and demonstrate the required financial capacity.

Legal & trademark

Trademark rights, contractual issues, objections and other legal considerations may affect an application.

Registry Service Provider

An applicant should assess the registry services and operational arrangements needed to run the TLD.

Community & geographic names

Specific applications may involve additional requirements, support or evaluation processes.

Objections & evaluation

Applicants should understand the applicable evaluation, comment, objection, appeal and contention procedures.

This overview intentionally avoids reproducing detailed requirements or fees. Always consult the current ICANN Applicant Guidebook and associated program documentation.

07 · FOR BRAND OWNERS

The .BRAND opportunity

A dotBrand is a brand-oriented top-level domain operated for the purposes permitted under the applicable ICANN rules. For a corporate applicant, the project can involve governance, legal and trademark analysis, technical operations, security, registry services and long-term domain-name policy.

Examples of potential naming structures illustrate the concept without implying any particular company’s plans:

  • www.company.brand
  • investor.company.brand
  • careers.company.brand
